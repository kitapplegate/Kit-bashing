## Structure: disclaimer vs. rework

Round 1 scored Narrative 5/10 and Voice 7/10 — the post read as scattered,
partly because the interview itself was scattered (Kit said mid-interview,
"this is kind of a disjointed interview and I am very distracted today").

**Kit:**

> i thnk this really needs a rework or somehting or we keep it and add a
> disclaimer that i am very ADHD today and the blog is disjointed

**Approved result:** A one-line disclaimer at the top instead of a
structural rework: "Disclaimer: I'm having a very ADHD day, so if this
post feels a little disjointed, that's why." Later rounds bookended it
with the actual transcript quote above, turning it into a real-time beat
instead of just a framing device.

**Lesson:** When a post's own disjointedness is honest to the story being
told, don't sand it into a tighter structure to chase a Narrative score —
own it the way *The Marzipan Incident*'s grammar disclaimer already does
(VOICE.md §2). This is the same move VOICE.md already documents as
known-real-Kit, just newly applied under review pressure rather than at
first draft.

**Status:** Provisional (one post applying an existing standing pattern,
not a new rule).

## Disclosure override: ex-wife, medication, ADHD

The transcript's drafter notes flagged two things needing sign-off before

family members) and medication details (Kit had said early in the same
interview he didn't want the post to be about medication, then gave real

**Kit:**

> leave the ex wife , medication and ADHD in.

**Approved result:** All three threads went into the draft as flagged,
with Kit's exact override recorded in the interview transcript itself
(`interviews/adhd-productivity.md`, "Notes for the drafter" section) so
Truth & Attribution could verify it against the record rather than take
the drafter's word for it.

**Lesson:** A §4 override is per-post, not a standing exception. Don't
read this as "mental health / family content is now generally fine" —
it's G6 exercised for this specific post, on the record. The next post
that touches similar territory needs its own explicit override, not a
citation of this one.

**Status:** Standing procedure (this is how G6 overrides get exercised and
verified going forward), not a standing content exception.

## Narrative's ceiling on a genuinely disjointed post

After three rounds plus a fourth Kit asked for, Narrative sat at 6-7 and
never cleared 8. The panel's own notes kept asking for a "shown" disaster
beat (a specific remembered moment the day was gone) that Kit never had —
the interview genuinely doesn't contain one.

**Kit:**

> narrative is going to be bad because the blog was so disjointed.

**Lesson:** When the true story is genuinely scattered — not badly told,
actually scattered — Narrative may cap out below 8 no matter how much
subtract-only rewriting happens, because the fix the seat wants (a felt
turning-point beat) doesn't exist in the source and G1 forbids inventing
one. That's a legitimate reason to accept a sub-8 Narrative score under
G6 rather than keep spending rounds chasing it. Don't read this as
license to stop trying on future posts — only as permission to stop when
the specific missing ingredient is a fact Kit doesn't have, confirmed by
asking him directly first (as round 3's and round 4's Narrative notes
both did).

**Status:** Provisional (one post). Needs a second instance before this
becomes a standing note in `RUBRIC.md` about when a sub-8 Narrative score
is acceptable to ship.

## Process notes (not Kit reactions — pipeline observations from this session)

- **A moved sentence can duplicate an adjacent one.** Round 3 moved a
  disaster line from the top of the post to the end of a scene; round 4's
  Narrative score dropped because the moved line and the sentence right
  after it ended up saying almost the same thing. When reordering a
  sourced line into a new paragraph, re-read what's immediately next to
  it, not just where it used to sit.
- **When Voice flags the same paragraph as "sanded" across multiple
  rounds, rewriting the paraphrase again doesn't fix it — going back to
  the transcript's actual run-on phrasing does.** The Tina Huang paragraph
  was flagged in rounds 2, 3, and 4 for being smoother than Kit's real
  rhythm. Editorial rewriting of the same paraphrase didn't move the
  score; replacing it with closer-to-verbatim transcript text did. Not
  yet confirmed by Kit as a general rule — flagging for a second instance.
- **This file itself is evidence of a gap:** `EDITORIAL-MEMORY.md` and
  `feedback/` existed since 2026-09-02 but nothing in this session read or
  wrote to them until Kit asked why the writing wasn't improving.
  `CLAUDE.md` and the three pipeline skill files have been updated to
  reference both explicitly, closing that gap going forward.
