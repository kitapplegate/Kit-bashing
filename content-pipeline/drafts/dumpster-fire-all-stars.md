---
title: 'Dumpster Fire All-Stars'
description: 'I decided AI gave me superpowers, built my fantasy league its own draft platform and its own AI commissioner, and three days before the real draft, exactly one of ten seats is filled — mine.'
pubDate: 'Aug 26 2026'
---

My fantasy football league has an AI commissioner. It does not have
a way to remember which invite code I sent to which friend.

I've always wanted to run a keeper league. Doing a lot of AI stuff —
getting into it, having it build things for me — I started thinking
I had superpowers now. Not "let's put together some analytics for
the draft we already do." The actual thought was: I can just build
the whole platform myself, host it myself, make it exactly how I
want it. It'll be super easy. Barely an inconvenience.

I'm doing this by myself — not a team, just me and Claude, and it's
kind of the blind leading the blind. Claude's got a pretty good beat
on things, but a lot of the quality-of-life stuff is glaring right
when you need it. Buttons sitting on top of each other. Things you
can't get done from a phone. And I'm three days from my draft date,
still finding it.

Here's the one that actually got me. There's a commissioner page
with a list of invite codes, one per open seat. You copy a code,
send it to a friend, they log in, type it in, and it fills their
slot. What that page does not do is remember which code went to
which person. So I sent them all out, and now I'm staring at a list
of codes that all look the same, hoping everyone still has the
original email. I was dumb. I should have done it better. But I
didn't.

I'm also a player in this league, and I own one of the teams, so I
didn't want the commissioner to also be one of the guys with skin in
the game. So I built an AI commissioner instead. It runs the league,
and if I ever need to step in, I can, because I gave myself all the
permissions.

I don't know how to build a fantasy football platform. What I know
how to do now is tell something that does, and let it go. Even the
kill -9 drill was Claude. I was completely hands off. That's most of
my coding. I'm not actually coding. It's Claude. And it worked — no
warning, the process just gone, and about five seconds later a brand
new copy of it was running, healthy. Nobody's tried killing it
mid-draft yet, with real picks on the board. That one's still on the
list before Saturday.

Claude even named the league. I asked for something funny, and it
came back with "Dumpster Fire All-Stars." I laughed the second I saw
it. Yeah. That's what it is. This is a dumpster fire right now.

The draft is Saturday. As of right now, one of the ten seats is
taken. That's it. Just me, all by myself. A couple of nephews are in
this league, some friends of friends, ten teams total. I pitched it,
and people said "yeah, send me the link, I'm definitely in" — and
then just didn't sign up. We were supposed to run a full mock draft
the night before this. I called it before it even started, didn't
launch it. Kind of boring, honestly — I just saw nobody was signing
up for that one either, so I called it. If Saturday comes and seats
are still empty, the platform autopicks for them. A bot drafts a
real team for a person who never showed up, and if that person logs
in later, they don't get the team they wanted. They get whatever the
bot already took.

Tonight, another email, texts to whoever I have a number for, maybe
a call to my nephew, and then a lot of praying people actually sign
up.

If there's one thing in this whole mess I'm actually proud of, it's
the data. NFL stats and draft rankings aren't free by default, and I
don't have the kind of money it takes to pay for that, so keeping
this thing free-99 was the whole game. Turns out there's a free
project called [nflverse](https://nflverse.nflverse.com/) that just
republishes the NFL's own data, and that's where all the players are
coming from. I'd show someone
that before I showed them anything else. Then I'd show them the
chaos.

Normally I build all of this at my desk at home — curved monitor, my
chair, a little dog bed next to it where my dogs lie down and keep
me company while I work. Right now I'm at my dad's, in a side room,
on a laptop, texting people to sign up.

If it works Saturday, if enough real people show up and the thing I
built with an AI just runs, I think I'll be proud of it either way,
recognition or not. And if it doesn't, It can only get better from
here.
