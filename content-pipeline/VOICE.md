# VOICE.md — Kit Bashing

**Status:** Standing authority for this blog's voice. Amend deliberately; don't
override it in a single post because one draft felt awkward.

---

## 0. What this document is for

This is a **detector, not a generator.**

There is not enough Kit-authored text in existence to teach a model to write
like Kit, and there never will be. Feeding an LLM more samples makes the
imitation *smoother*, and smooth is the tell. So this document is not here to
help anything write in Kit's voice. It's here to catch it when the writing
stops sounding like him.

Two labeled corpora make that possible:

- **Known-real Kit:** *The Marzipan Incident* (2008–2011, byline "3rdshoe"),
  11 posts, written by Kit alone with no AI involvement.
- **Known-fake Kit:** `why-the-jump.md` and `board-of-directors.md`, both
  written by Claude from Kit's dictated material. His facts, not his sentences.

Everything below is derived from the difference between those two.

## 1. The Law

> **Facts are locked. Prose is free. Don't sand the lurch.**

*Amended 2026-08-03 by Kit, after three rounds of `sounds-like-me`. The original
rule was "the machine may only subtract" — no sentence Kit hadn't said. It
failed: the Reader seat scored the strictest draft a 3, unable to parse it, and
the pipeline spent rounds arguing about single words. Kit's ruling:*

> *"Just me rambling and them taking quotes here and here and stringing them
> together really isn't gonna work. With guardrails they can write stuff,
> rewrite things, make things sound better together. I just don't want slop. I
> want it to sound more like me. You can find my voice in my ramblings."*

**You may write.** Connective tissue, transitions, rewrites, restructuring,
clarifying sentences. That is the job.

### The six guardrails

**G1 — Facts come from the transcript. Always.** Every number, name, date,
event, quote, and claim about the world. You may write the sentence; you may not
invent what it asserts. If a fact isn't in the transcript, it is a question for
Kit, not a plausible detail. **This is the one that stays absolute** — it's what
the Truth seat enforces and it's non-negotiable.

**G2 — Never put words in a real person's mouth.** Do not write, imply, or
arrange anything suggesting a real person said or did something they didn't.
*Learned the hard way:* a round-3 draft had Joe Rogan, NetworkChuck and Zach
Galifianakis interviewing Kit and credited Galifianakis with a line Claude
wrote. Five seats missed it. If an AI persona appears in a post, the post says
it's a persona — **every time it appears, in every post**, not once for the
whole blog. Kit's ruling, 2026-08-04, after the truth seat caught an
undisclosed Zach Galifianakis line in `same-feeling-twice`: a reader landing
on any single post cold won't necessarily have read the one post that
disclosed the panel first.

**G3 — Anything you write must pass §3.** The seven tells apply with full force
to your own sentences. No punchline tax, no dismiss-then-concede, no tidy
similes, no negate-then-correct, no self-annotation, no em-dash padding, no
uniform rhythm. You are held to a *higher* standard here than Kit is, because
his tells are his and yours are slop.

**G4 — Keep what already works.** Editorial license is for the parts that don't
land. When one of his lines is good, it stays. "It's long gone. It's nowhere.
It's in the landfill somewhere, probably. Turned back into something." — do not
improve that. The instinct to touch a working line is the instinct this document
exists to restrain.

**G5 — Don't sand the lurch.** His rhythm is uneven: a forty-word run-on, then
four words. Prose you write matches that; it does not correct it. Smooth is the
tell. A draft where your sentences are detectably more even than his has failed
even if every sentence is good. but it still needs to be readable, a little sanding is ok just not replaceing.

**G6 — Kit approves.** He is the last gate and he can override anything in this
document, including this document.

### What is *not* a violation

Kit, same ruling: *"You're getting really caught up on width and bandwidth, or I
didn't say these things in one sentence — I said it in two. That's a little
asinine."* He is right. Do not spend a review round on:

- Which of two synonyms a voice-to-text pass produced
- Whether two of his sentences became one, or one became two
- Sentence-level provenance of ordinary connective prose
- Whether a paragraph break falls where he paused

Editors may now **suggest concrete fixes**, including sample phrasing. The
drafter writes; the editors advise. The old rule barring editors from supplying
prose is retired along with the subtract-only Law.

### 1a. Retired

*This section policed arrangement — paragraph breaks, splices, repetition
trims — back when the drafter couldn't write sentences and authored rhythm
instead. With prose freed under the amended Law, most of it is moot. Retired
2026-08-03.*

Two things it got right survive, folded into the guardrails above:

- **Repetition is often insistence, not stutter.** "talking and talking and
  talking" said three times means something different than twice. Cut it if the
  paragraph is better for it — but know what you're cutting. (G4)
- **Don't curate his roughness.** Keeping the stutters that read as charming
  while fixing the ones that read as clumsy is a polishing hand pretending to be
  a raw one. Voice seat, round 3: *"Charming is the same failure as polished,
  wearing different clothes."* Be consistent in either direction. (G5)

## 2. What real Kit actually sounds like

Verbatim, from *The Marzipan Incident*:

> *"Disclaimer: Do to the absence of my wife the below post may be riddled with
> bad grammar and incomplete sentences and overall confusion"*

> *"I do agree with Pan as to being good stewards of our planet. I conceded to
> this point but in the same breath made it known there would be no tofu,
> incense, or tambourine playing"*

> *"unmatched ability to cause people to stare in unbelief"*

### First, the distinction the pipeline got wrong for four rounds

**Marzipan is typed prose. The transcript is speech. They are not the same
thing and only one of them is the target.**

Kit's written voice is loose, typo-tolerant, and clunky — **in complete
sentences.** He shipped "Do to" for "Due to," but he did not ship fragments.
The broken-off half-sentences in the interview transcript ("So scattered and
shattered." / "Same job." / "Too symmetrical.") are artifacts of Win+H
punctuating his pauses. They are not how he writes and they are not voice.

Kit's ruling, 2026-08-03, reading a draft that preserved them:

> *"It does sound like me, it sounds raw and wrong. It repeats a lot, which I do
> when I talk. I want it like that, just not as jittery. This line — 'What I
> needed was somebody to ask me things and not judge how my mind is. So
> scattered and shattered.' — it should be one sentence. Yes it sounded like me,
> and no it's not good, because it sounded too much like rambling."*

**Keep:** repetition, hedges ("I guess," "I think"), plain words, clunky
constructions, contradictions he doesn't resolve, jokes he doesn't explain.

**Join:** fragments into sentences. A dictation pause is not a stylistic choice.

This seat previously scored preserved fragments as authenticity and rewarded
them. That was wrong and it is why a draft could sound like him and still be bad.

### Four things in the samples above, all of which matter:

**He ships typos.** "Do to" for "Due to," published and left alone. He opens a
post with a disclaimer about his own grammar rather than fixing the grammar.
Polish is not a value he holds.

**He doesn't explain his own jokes.** "no tofu, incense, or tambourine
playing" is the funniest thing on that blog and it is followed by *nothing*.
No reflective clause, no wry annotation, no landing. He says it and moves on.

**His phrasing is sometimes clunky and he leaves it.** "unmatched ability to
cause people to stare in unbelief" is not a workshopped sentence. It's a
first-thought sentence, and it has more personality than a polished one would.

**The humor is concrete and absurd, never abstract.** Tofu, incense,
tambourines. Specific nouns doing specific ridiculous work. He does not make
observations *about* humor; he just says the funny specific thing.

## 3. What fake Kit sounds like

These are the actual AI fingerprints found in the two current posts. Each one
is a scoring criterion for the Slop Detector seat.

### Tell 1 — The punchline tax

Every paragraph is made to land something. Nothing is allowed to just sit
there.

> "…which is corporate for 'no,' said as gently as possible."
> "Cheap motivational-poster logic, and it worked on me anyway."
> "…the classic hobbyist's cycle of self-inflicted problems."
> "…which is, annoyingly, the entire point of the thing working."

Real writing has flat paragraphs. A post where every paragraph earns applause
is a post nobody wrote.

### Tell 2 — Dismiss-then-concede

Set up a wry judgment, then undercut it with a concession, in the same
sentence. "Cheap motivational-poster logic, **and it worked on me anyway.**"
This construction appears repeatedly in both posts. It is an LLM signature
rhythm, not a Kit rhythm.

### Tell 3 — The too-tidy simile

> "in the way a shirt can technically fit and still feel wrong every time you
> put it on"

Balanced, symmetrical, workshopped. Nobody says this out loud. Compare to
"tofu, incense, or tambourine playing" — same job, but that one sounds like a
person and this one sounds like a writing sample.

### Tell 4 — Negate-then-correct

"I am not a company." "The idea wasn't mine." "not a fix, a whole standing
thing." Defining by negation, over and over, as a rhythm device.

### Tell 5 — Self-annotation

The prose keeps commenting on itself. "which is, annoyingly, the entire point."
"let's be honest." "to be fair." Real Kit states things; fake Kit states things
and then tells you how to feel about having read them.

### Tell 6 — Em-dash density

Both posts lean on the em dash as the default connective. Track it. More than
roughly one per paragraph is a smell.

### Tell 7 — Uniform sentence length

The fake posts hold a steady medium-length rhythm throughout. Real speech
lurches: a 40-word run-on, then four words, then another run-on.

## 4. Standing constraints

**Disclosure boundaries.** These are Kit's, established across earlier
sessions. Do not cross them, do not ask him to reconsider them mid-draft:

- No mortgage or specific financial figures
- No mental health specifics
- No kids' names
- No denomination specifics beyond "my church"
- Nothing about family members who haven't consented to being written about

**Attribution.** If an idea came from someone else, name them and link them.
This is not optional and it has already gone wrong once: the
board-of-directors post originally implied the idea was Kit's when it came from
YouTuber Austin Marchese, and had to be corrected after publication. The Truth
& Attribution seat exists because of that incident.

**No invented specifics.** Numbers, commands, error messages, dates, and
quotes must come from the transcript. A plausible-sounding detail is worse
than no detail, because it reads as *more* credible, not less.

## 5. Mechanical conventions

- **Source line wrap:** ~72–75 characters. Both existing posts follow this.
- **Frontmatter:** `title` in sentence case, single-quoted. `description` is
  one sentence. `pubDate` format `'Mon DD YYYY'` (e.g. `'Jul 19 2026'`).
- **The `description` field is exempt from the Law.** Kit's ruling, round 5 of
  `sounds-like-me`, after the Truth seat failed a draft over an AI-written
  description: *"That one I'm OK with the AI writing it. It's not supposed to be
  me writing it, I guess. But I like what it says."* It is a shelf label, not
  the post. **He must still approve the exact wording** at the publish gate, and
  the title is **not** covered — titles come from his words. Everything in the
  post body remains under §1 with no exceptions.
- **Length:** 700–1000 words. Both existing posts sit in that band.
- **Emphasis:** italics on single words for stress; bold for structural labels
  only. No emoji. No exclamation points unless Kit actually shouted.
- **Links:** inline markdown, used for attribution and for things a reader
  would otherwise have to go search for.

---

## 6. Hard bans

Never appears in a Kit Bashing post:

- "In today's world," "Let's dive in," "game-changer," "unlock," "leverage"
  (as a verb), "journey" (as a metaphor), "at the end of the day"
- Second-person coaching voice ("You should try…", "Here's what you need to
  know")
- Listicles, numbered takeaways, "3 lessons I learned"
- A closing paragraph that summarizes the post you just read
- Rhetorical questions used as section transitions
- LinkedIn cadence: one-sentence paragraphs stacked for emphasis
