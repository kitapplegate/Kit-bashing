# Editorial memory

**Status:** Distilled lessons from Kit's actual approvals, rejections, and
overrides. Read this before every blog session.

This file is the blog's durable memory. It does not replace `VOICE.md`, the
facts in an interview transcript, or Kit's judgment. It records what the
writing process has learned from him so a new session does not have to make the
same mistakes again.

## Authority

When guidance conflicts, use this order:

1. Facts and disclosure boundaries in the current interview
2. Kit's current explicit direction and G6 final authority
3. `VOICE.md` and `RUBRIC.md`
4. Standing decisions in this file
5. Provisional patterns and older examples

Never turn a reviewer preference into a rule merely because it received a
score. Kit's reaction is the learning signal.

## Standing decisions

### Ordinary product mentions are not attribution claims

Naming a familiar product or service in firsthand context does not, by itself,
require a link. Attribution is required when the post relies on somebody
else's idea, reporting, research, quote, or factual claim. Add a product link
when it gives the reader useful context, not to satisfy a mechanical name
check.

**Source:** `feedback/four-more-hours-of-lunch.md`, where Kit rejected a Truth
veto over the phrase "Google account."

## How to capture feedback

Each `feedback/<slug>.md` record should include:

- The stage where the feedback happened
- The exact wording Kit rejected or questioned
- Kit's response in his own words
- The approved replacement or final decision
- A narrow lesson for the next draft
- Whether the lesson is provisional or standing

Positive reactions matter too. Record what Kit approved so future drafts do
not "improve" it back into something he disliked.

## Maintenance

- Add feedback during the session while the reason is still clear.
- After each published post, check whether any provisional pattern now has
  evidence from a second post.
- Keep this file distilled; move examples and history to `feedback/`.
- If Kit reverses a preference, keep the old decision in its post record and
  update the standing rule with the newer decision.
- Do not infer a preference from silence or from publication alone.
